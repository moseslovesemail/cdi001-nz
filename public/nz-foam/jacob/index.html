How to use the radar

Six steps to find and track spray foam opportunities.

Step 1: Review and filter

Browse the regional lists. Use filters: High fit (score 75+), Qualify (55–74), Monitor (below 55), Unreviewed, or Reviewed to find relevant projects.

Step 2: Open the project

Click a project to see verified facts: consent reference, description, stage, published value, and source links. Check sourced organisations and contact routes where loaded.

Step 3: Verify organisation and contact

Confirm the applicant or contact person is current. General business phone or inbox may only route to the responsible person — call to verify. Applicant is not necessarily the decision-maker.

Step 4: Call or email

Reach out to the builder, project manager or architect. Confirm who owns the insulation package and whether decisions remain open. Edit the suggested outreach before sending — no automated email is provided.

Step 5: Record your action

Mark: Relevant / Watch / Not relevant. Set Owner, Contacted Yes/No, Plans received Yes/No, and Quote value in NZD. Data saves locally.

Step 6: Record outcome and export

Set Outcome to Open / Won / Lost / No fit. If Won, record revenue won only after the contract is confirmed. Add notes for next action or reasons. Export reviews CSV to share with your team.

Important reminders

Data is stored locally

Reviews are saved in your browser only. If you clear your browser data, reviews are lost. No data sync between devices. Export regularly to CSV.

No automatic financial deductions

Quote pipeline is your sum of quote values you entered. Revenue won is what you record after confirmed contract award. No invoicing or deductions occur automatically.

Consent ≠ specification

A building consent means the project exists. It does not mean spray foam is specified, suitable, or will be chosen. Always verify with plans and the builder.

Fit score is indicative

The score reflects description-based rules, not confirmed product fit or guaranteed suitability. Manual review of each project before quoting is essential.
